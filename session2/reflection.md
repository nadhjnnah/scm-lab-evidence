Session 2 Reflection



Q1. How do Issues and pull requests together support change control?



Before making changes, the team uses issues to document requirements, challenges, and expected results. Pull requests give developers a chance to suggest changes and describe their work. Before merging, team members can check the modifications and suggest recommendations. As a result, the process of transformation becomes controlled and structured.



Q2. Why did the conflict in Part 4 happen, and how could a team reduce such conflicts?



Two branches changed the same line in the same file in different ways, which caused the conflict. The deployment target was modified in one branch to staging and in another to production. Git was unable to identify the correct version. Proper communication, pulling the most recent changes, and avoiding from updating the same parts at the same time can all help a team reduce conflict.



Q3. What would be lost if people merged straight into main without a pull request?



The chance to look at and discuss modifications before to their merge would be missed. Errors and possible issues may go unnoticed for Also, less decisions and input would be documented. Pull requests improve the tracking of changes and enhance code quality.
